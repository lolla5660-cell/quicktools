Compound Interest Calculator

Estimate how your savings or investment could grow over time with compound interest and regular contributions.

What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original principal and the interest that has accumulated over time. This can allow money to grow faster as interest is added to the balance and can itself earn additional interest.

How to Use the Compound Interest Calculator

Enter your initial investment, monthly contribution, estimated annual interest rate and investment period. Then choose how often the interest is compounded and select Calculate.

Compound Interest Formula

For a single initial investment with periodic compounding, the basic compound interest formula is:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

In this formula, A is the final amount, P is the initial principal, r is the annual interest rate expressed as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the number of years.

This calculator also accounts for regular monthly contributions, making it useful for estimating the effect of consistent saving or investing over time.

Example

Suppose you start with $1,000, contribute $100 each month, earn an estimated 5% annual interest rate, and leave the money invested for 10 years. The calculator can estimate your ending balance and show how much came from your contributions versus growth.

Compound Interest vs. Simple Interest

With simple interest, interest is generally calculated only on the original principal. With compound interest, previously earned interest is added to the balance and can earn additional interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on the original amount plus accumulated interest.

How often can interest be compounded?

Interest may be compounded annually, semi-annually, quarterly, monthly or daily depending on the account or investment.

Does this calculator guarantee investment returns?

No. The results are estimates based on the values you enter. Actual investment returns can vary and may be affected by fees, taxes, market conditions and other factors.
